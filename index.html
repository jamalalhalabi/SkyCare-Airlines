01 · The customer problem

Airports Have Information. Passengers Still Get Lost.

A single connection can mean checking a dozen separate systems. Each one works. Together, they leave the passenger to connect the dots under time pressure.

?
SkyCareNavigator
  • Airline app
  • Boarding pass
  • Airport map
  • Departure boards
  • Baggage screens
  • Airport signs
  • Restaurant directory
  • Translation app
  • Accessibility service
  • Information desk
  • Emergency numbers
  • Airline customer service

Today: twelve disconnected sources, one stressed passenger.

The information exists. The problem is that the passenger has to find it, understand it, and decide what to do next.

That is the customer-service problem SkyCare solves.

How SkyCare works

The passenger should not have to figure out where to go, who to ask, or what to do next.

  1. 1

    Import the trip

    Boarding pass or reservation. SkyCare learns flights, gates, bags, and needs once.

  2. 2

    Understand context

    Time left, distance, gate changes, accessibility, family, connectivity.

  3. 3

    Guide in the moment

    One next step at a time, with an AR-style route drawn on the floor.

  4. 4

    Hand off to people

    When judgment, care, or an emergency is involved, SkyCare gets you to the right human.

Great customer service is not only solving problems after they happen. It is reducing confusion before the passenger needs to ask for help.

    Medical & emergency assistance

    In an Emergency, No One Should Search a Directory.

    When a passenger says "I don't feel well," SkyCare puts medical help first and hides everything else.

    Prototype simulation · no one is contacted

    Select an option. In a real emergency, tell the nearest airport or airline employee immediately and call the local emergency number (112 in Türkiye).

    This website never contacts emergency responders. In production, emergency and medical requests would require direct integration with participating airport authorities and emergency systems.

    Family Guardian · Inspired by a class scenario

    Tickets Bought Together. Seats Split Apart.

    An adult is traveling with two young children. The booking is together, but the seat assignments are not. SkyCare catches it before boarding, not at the aircraft door.

    FAMILY SEATING ISSUE DETECTED

    Young travelers on this reservation may not be seated adjacent to their accompanying adult.

    FAMILY SEATING RESOLVED

    The airline reassigned the family to 14A, 14B, and 14C. (Demo outcome)

    Contact Airline Get Help

    SkyCare cannot change airline seat inventory. It identifies the issue early and directs the passenger toward the airline's resolution options. In the U.S., DOT proposed a rule in 2024 that would require fee-free adjacent seating for young children (U.S. Department of Transportation, 2024).

      Service quality, perceived value, and satisfaction shape whether passengers return and recommend a service (Chen, 2008; Park et al., 2004). SkyCare competes on the moments passengers remember most.

      Research foundation

      Built on Evidence, Not Assumptions.

      Every major feature responds to a finding from peer-reviewed or government sources.

      Research finding

      In an analysis of more than 1,000 online airport reviews, confusing signage and poor wayfinding were among the main sources of passenger dissatisfaction, alongside security and dining (Bogicevic et al., 2013).

      How SkyCare applies it

      Guidance moves from overhead signs to the passenger's screen, one next step at a time.

      Research finding

      Terminal wayfinding quality can be measured through decision points and level changes, and those measures were validated against passenger surveys (Churchill et al., 2008).

      How SkyCare applies it

      AR prompts appear before each decision point and level change, where passengers are most likely to get lost.

      Research finding

      In a wheelchair-simulator study of an airport terminal, a mobile wayfinding app did not make trips faster, but it sharply reduced time spent reading signs and lowered users' anxiety (Qing et al., 2021).

      How SkyCare applies it

      SkyCare's accessible route aims to lower stress and mental effort. We do not claim it makes passengers faster.

      Research finding

      Older adults with visual or cognitive decline struggle to orient in airports; smartphone tools look promising, but many design guidelines lack scientific testing (Bosch & Gharaveis, 2017).

      How SkyCare applies it

      Large, simple prompts and human handoff are built in, and SkyCare would need user testing with older travelers before launch.

      Research finding

      Passengers differ in whether they choose self-service technology; not every traveler wants to do it themselves (Castillo-Manzano & López-Valpuesta, 2013).

      How SkyCare applies it

      "Talk to a person" is always one tap away. Technology is an option, never a wall.

      Research finding

      After a service failure, fair outcomes, a fair process, and respectful treatment all shape how passengers judge the recovery (Migacz et al., 2018). Among delayed passengers, both thinking and emotions shaped satisfaction with the recovery (Wen & Chi, 2013).

      How SkyCare applies it

      Rush Mode and help routing cut uncertainty fast and hand passengers to the right person with the right information.

      Government source

      U.S. DOT states that passengers with disabilities have a right to prompt assistance moving through the airport, including between gates on connections (U.S. Department of Transportation, 2025).

      How SkyCare applies it

      The assistance timeline makes sure the request follows the passenger from departure to arrival.
